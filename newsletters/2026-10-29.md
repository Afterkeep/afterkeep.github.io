## This week's tip: write down your subscriptions, not just your accounts

Once an Australian bank is told about a death, it secures accounts in the person's sole name and restricts debits, but some card payments can still go through. Streaming, music, cloud storage and app subscriptions are easy to miss. Spend ten minutes listing every paid service you have, which card or account it bills, and whether it's billed through a phone or internet provider instead. Keep the list with your estate documents (no passwords in it), and tell your executor where it is.

If you're the one sorting things out, CommBank says it can give the estate representative a list of regular payments on the deceased's debit card, so it's worth asking your bank for the same.

## Worth knowing: Yahoo's deceased-user process is US-only

If someone in your family had a Yahoo Mail account, this one matters for Australians. According to Yahoo's help page:

- Yahoo won't provide passwords or login details, and all Yahoo accounts are non-transferable, even after the owner dies.
- Closing the account, or cancelling billing and premium services, needs a copy of a death certificate issued in the United States.
- Getting the account's content needs the death certificate, the requester's government ID, and a US court order that meets Yahoo's requirements.
- The page says it applies to US accounts only, and requests for non-US accounts won't be accepted or answered.

For an Australian account, check the regional Yahoo Help site, and stop any Yahoo charges through the bank. The bigger lesson: if you want something kept from a Yahoo inbox, forward or export it while you still can.

Source: [Options available if a Yahoo account owner passes away (Yahoo Help)](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/account/sln2021.html). Checked 29 October 2026.

## Featured tool: Apple Legacy Contact

*Editor's pick, not sponsored. Afterkeep isn't paid for this mention.*

If you use an iPhone, iPad or Mac, this free setting is the easiest way to let someone you trust get to your photos, messages, notes, files and device backups after you die. On iPhone it's in Settings, then your name, then Sign-In & Security, then Legacy Contact. You can add more than one person, and they don't need an Apple device or Apple Account. To request access they'll need the access key you generate and your death certificate. It doesn't cover iCloud Keychain passwords and passkeys, or movies, music and books you bought.
