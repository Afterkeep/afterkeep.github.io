## This week's tip: tell one person where to look

You don't need a perfect plan. Spend ten minutes writing down where your important accounts live (email, phone, photos, bank, super) and tell one trusted person where that note is kept. Don't write passwords in it; use a password manager's emergency access or each service's own legacy tool instead.

## Worth knowing: what Apple's Legacy Contact can and can't reach

Apple's support page says a Legacy Contact is someone you choose to have access to certain data in your Apple Account after your death:

- Data may include photos, messages, notes, files and device backups.
- It does not include purchased films, music, books or subscriptions, or iCloud Keychain data (payment information, passwords and passkeys).
- To request access they need the access key you generated and your death certificate.
- They don't need an Apple Account or Apple device. Any one Legacy Contact can individually decide about your data, including permanently deleting it.

Source: [How to add a Legacy Contact for your Apple Account (Apple Support AU)](https://support.apple.com/en-au/102631). Checked 19 November 2026.
