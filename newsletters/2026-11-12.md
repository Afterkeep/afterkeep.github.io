## This week's tip: get what you need before you close anything

When someone dies it's tempting to start closing accounts straight away. Before you do, make a short list of what you actually want to keep, such as photos, emails and documents, and ask for that first. Closing is usually permanent, and some services can't hand anything over once an account is gone. Cancel paid subscriptions early to stop the charges, but leave account closure until last.

## Worth knowing: Google can't hand over content once an account is closed

Google's help page for deceased users' accounts says it can work with immediate family members and representatives to close an account where appropriate, and in certain circumstances may provide content. A few points from it:

- Google can't provide passwords or other login details.
- If you choose to close the account, Google is unable to process any request to turn over the contents later.
- If you've already asked for the contents, wait for that request to finish before submitting any other request.
- Every decision is made after a careful review, so approval isn't guaranteed.

So if you want the photos or emails, ask for the data first and close afterwards. Better still, if it's your own account, set up Inactive Account Manager so someone you trust is named in advance.

Source: [Submit a request regarding a deceased user's account (Google Account Help)](https://support.google.com/accounts/troubleshooter/6357590). Checked 12 November 2026.
